I work primarily with Go, Kubernetes control plane components, service mesh architectures, and eBPF-based instrumentation. Most of my time is spent thinking about reliability, performance, and how to make complex systems comprehensible to the people who operate them.

I also studied philosophy. My thesis examined Heidegger's account of technical practice and its relevance to software engineering. I'm interested in pragmatism, phenomenology, and what it means to think clearly about tools and craft.

This site is where I write about both sides of that work. The technical posts are usually deep-dives into infrastructure problems. The philosophy posts are slower and more exploratory.
